How to size a trade in 3 steps.

Step 1: decide your max risk in money. Example: a $1,000 account risking 1% per trade means a max loss of $10 on this trade.

Step 2: measure the distance to your stop. Entry at $20.00, stop at $19.50. That's $0.50 of risk per share.

Step 3: size = max risk ÷ stop distance. $10 ÷ $0.50 = 20 shares. If the stop is hit, you lose about $10 before fees and slippage.

Wider stop, smaller size. Tighter stop, larger size. A $1.00 stop means 10 shares, a $0.25 stop means 40. The dollar risk stays the same either way.

You choose the loss first. Save this calculator logic, and comment "size" if you'll use it on your next trade.
