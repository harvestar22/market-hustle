His first real loss felt personal. It wasn't.

Four wins in a row, then one red bar, and it felt like an insult.

But losses are part of any edge. Even good strategies have red days. The market wasn't punishing him.

Taking it personally makes you trade to get even. That's when one loss becomes four.

The trade is not your worth. One is a position on a screen. The other is you. Keep them in different boxes.

Grade the decision, not the outcome. Did you follow the plan? Right size? Stop respected? That's the grade. A good trade can lose, and a bad trade can win. Over time, good process is what you can control.
