The 1% rule, explained in 6 pictures.

Risk 1% of the account per trade: on $5,000, that's $50 lost if the stop is hit.

The math is why it matters. Ten losses in a row at 1% leaves about 90% of the account (0.99^10 ≈ 0.904). The same streak at 10% leaves about 35% (0.9^10 ≈ 0.349), and you'd need roughly a 187% gain to get back to even.

Size comes from the stop: money at risk ÷ distance to the stop. $50 ÷ $0.50 = 100 shares. Lower than 1% is fine. Changing the number when you feel lucky or angry is the problem.
